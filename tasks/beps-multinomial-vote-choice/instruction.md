The dataset at /app/environment/data/BEPS.csv is the British Election Panel Study analysed by Fox and Andersen (2006): 1,525 respondents who each voted Conservative, Labour, or Liberal Democrat. Working in R, fit a single multinomial logistic regression of vote on the respondent's age, gender, the national and household economic assessments, the Blair, Hague and Kennedy leader ratings, the eleven-point Europe scale (higher means more Eurosceptic), and political knowledge on a zero-to-three scale, and add an interaction that lets the Europe effect vary with political knowledge. Treat every rating and scale column as numeric, take Conservative as the reference party, and do all of your work in /app/environment/analysis.R: the verifier wipes /app/environment/outputs and re-runs that one file from scratch each time it scores, so write every result into that directory and use no other entrypoint. The tables under data/reference are for orientation only.

Write three CSVs into /app/environment/outputs. coefficients.csv holds the raw coefficients of both non-reference equations with columns equation, term, estimate, std_error, z, p_value, where equation is labour or libdem and term is one of intercept, age, gender_male, econ_national, econ_household, blair, hague, kennedy, europe, political_knowledge or europe_x_political_knowledge. marginal_effects.csv holds the average marginal effect of each predictor on each party's probability with columns outcome, predictor, ame, ame_se, ame_z, ame_p, across the outcomes Conservative, Labour and Liberal Democrat and the nine predictors above (no intercept or interaction term); average each effect over all respondents and pair it with a delta-method standard error, using the instantaneous marginal effect (the slope of the predicted probability, not a one-unit change) for the continuous predictors and the discrete switch from the female to the male level for gender. Form each delta-method standard error by taking the Jacobian of the average marginal effect with respect to the fitted coefficient vector by centered finite difference with step size 1e-4, perturbing each fitted coefficient up then down by 1e-4 and recomputing the average marginal effect each time, and combining that Jacobian J with the model variance-covariance matrix V from vcov as the square root of the diagonal of J times V times the transpose of J. lr_tests.csv holds a likelihood-ratio test with columns term, df, lr_chisq, p_value for each of age, gender_male, econ_national, econ_household, blair, hague, kennedy and europe_x_political_knowledge, each comparing the full model against the model with only that one term dropped. Sort every file in ascending lexicographic order by its label columns (equation then term; outcome then predictor; term), comparing the label strings character by character rather than by any factor-level or domain order.

Quantify the Europe-by-knowledge interaction on the probability scale rather than the log-odds scale, and write /app/environment/outputs/europe_knowledge_interaction.json. The raw interaction coefficient is not this quantity: what is wanted is how the average instantaneous marginal effect of the Europe scale on each party's predicted probability changes between politically unaware and politically aware respondents. Compute it as a counterfactual on the whole sample, not by subsetting. First set political_knowledge to zero for every respondent, leaving all other covariates at their observed values, and take the average over respondents of the instantaneous Europe marginal effect on each party probability, that is the centered finite difference of the average predicted probability when the Europe scale is moved up then down by 1e-4 with the europe-by-knowledge product recomputed accordingly; write these as ame_europe_k0_conservative, ame_europe_k0_labour and ame_europe_k0_libdem. Then repeat with political_knowledge set to three for every respondent and write ame_europe_k3_conservative, ame_europe_k3_labour and ame_europe_k3_libdem. The interaction effect for each party, interaction_effect_conservative, interaction_effect_labour and interaction_effect_libdem, is the knowledge-three average marginal effect minus the knowledge-zero one; the three interaction effects sum to zero because the probabilities sum to one. Round each to six decimals as a bare number.

Then summarise what Euroscepticism does to the national result as a whole-electorate counterfactual rather than as a local slope, and write /app/environment/outputs/europe_first_difference.json with keys share_europhile_conservative, share_europhile_labour, share_europhile_libdem, share_eurosceptic_conservative, share_eurosceptic_labour, share_eurosceptic_libdem, first_diff_conservative, first_diff_labour and first_diff_libdem. Imagine moving every respondent to the most pro-European end of the eleven-point scale while leaving each respondent's other characteristics, including their political knowledge, untouched, and report the share_europhile values as the predicted vote shares this implies, that is the average across all respondents of each party's predicted probability under that counterfactual. Then imagine the opposite electorate, every respondent moved to the most Eurosceptic end of the scale, and report the share_eurosceptic values the same way. This is a discrete first difference across the full span of the scale, not the instantaneous marginal effect you reported above, and it is a population average rather than a prediction at the average respondent, so changing the Europe position must flow through every part of the fitted model that depends on it before the probabilities are averaged. Give each first_diff as the eurosceptic share minus the europhile share for that party, so the three first differences sum to zero, and round every value to six decimals as a bare number.

Finally, write /app/environment/outputs/model_fit.json with the keys n, n_parameters, log_likelihood, deviance, aic, mcfadden_r2, accuracy, multiclass_brier, interaction_lr_chisq, interaction_df and interaction_p, and save five PNGs in the same directory: effect_europe_by_knowledge.png for the predicted party probabilities across the Europe scale at each political-knowledge level, ame_forest.png for the marginal effects by party with intervals, vote_shares.png for the observed vote shares, calibration.png for predicted versus observed frequencies, and confusion_heatmap.png for predicted against actual party. Round every number you write, in both the CSVs and the JSON, to six decimal places, and keep each JSON value a bare number rather than a quoted string.
