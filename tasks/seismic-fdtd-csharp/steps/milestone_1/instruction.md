We're starting a seismic-wave simulator in C# under /app/Seismic. The eventual goal is to model elastic-wave propagation through layered, faulted, and salt-bearing earth models for survey design and subsurface interpretation. This milestone delivers the model-and-source layer: a parser that rasterises a geological description onto a uniform grid, plus a source-time generator for Ricker, explosive, and vibroseis-sweep wavelets.

Build a .NET 8 project at /app/Seismic producing an executable invokable through /app/seismic, a shell wrapper that runs the built dll, marked executable. The `model` subcommand `seismic model <input.json> <output_dir>` writes five float32 NPY arrays, `vp.npy`, `vs.npy`, `rho.npy`, `qp.npy`, `qs.npy`, each of shape `(nz, nx)`, holding the per-cell P-wave velocity, S-wave velocity, density, and P/S quality factors. The cell at index `[iz, ix]` is centred at `(ix*dx, iz*dz)` measured from the top-left corner. Layers cover depth intervals looked up by cell-centre depth; salt-body polygons override the layered background by point-in-polygon test; faults shift the layer-lookup depth by their throw on the hanging-wall side. Faults apply before salt, so a cell inside a salt polygon keeps the salt properties even where a fault would otherwise have moved it, and when several faults list a cell on their hanging-wall side the throws add together. The full input schema, including the hanging-wall predicate and ordering rules, lives in /app/docs/model_schema.md.

The `source` subcommand `seismic source <input.json> <output.npy>` writes a 1D float32 NPY array of length `round(duration_s * sample_rate_hz)`, normalised so the maximum absolute value equals exactly 1. Support three wavelet types, `ricker`, `explosive`, and `vibroseis`, with their analytic forms documented at /app/docs/source_schema.md. NPY format details are at /app/docs/npy_format.md. Sample fixtures live at /app/fixtures/layered_*.json and /app/fixtures/source_*.json. Exit zero on success, non-zero on bad input.
