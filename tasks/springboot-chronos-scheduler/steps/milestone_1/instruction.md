The infra team at our company has been kicking around a homegrown Quartz replacement for months. The old Quartz cluster's licensing situation is messy, the team doesn't want a JVM-wide singleton scheduler in every service, and nobody on the platform team is excited about pulling `org.quartz` into the Spring Boot 3 services we're standardising on. The repo at `/app` is the scaffold one of the platform engineers started — Spring Boot 3 / Java 21 / H2-JDBC, no Quartz on the classpath and we want to keep it that way. The whole thing has to be hand-rolled.

The piece that's blocking everyone is the cron expression parser. We need the full Quartz dialect — 6 fields (`seconds minutes hours dayOfMonth month dayOfWeek`), `L` / `W` / `#` / `?`, names like `JAN` and `MON`, day-of-week numbered 1=SUN through 7=SAT the way Quartz does it rather than the way `java.time` does it. The mutual `?` rule between `dayOfMonth` and `dayOfWeek` has to be enforced — exactly one of them is `?`, never both `*` and never both `?`. Expressions like `0 15 10 ? * 6L` (last Friday of the month) and `0 15 10 ? * 6#3` (third Friday) and `0 0 12 LW * ?` (last weekday of the month) all need to round-trip. `nextExecutionTime` needs to do the right thing across DST boundaries in whatever zone the caller passes — both the spring-forward gap and the fall-back duplicate hour, neither of which the JDK gives you for free. When a rule lands in the fall-back overlap, where the same wall-clock time occurs twice, fire at the earlier of the two occurrences — the one before the clocks go back, i.e. at the earlier UTC offset.

The two endpoints under `/api/cron` already exist as controller shells. `POST /parse` returns `{"valid": true, "fields": [...]}` on success — a 6-element array, one entry per field, in canonical order, each entry shaped `{"name": <field-name>, "raw": <original-text>, "values": <sorted-int-list>, "special": <marker-or-null>}` where `name` is one of `SECONDS`, `MINUTES`, `HOURS`, `DAY_OF_MONTH`, `MONTH`, `DAY_OF_WEEK`. On failure it returns `{"valid": false, "error": <human-readable-reason>}`; for a wrong field count the error reason should literally mention "fields" so callers can grep for it. `POST /next` takes `{"expression", "from", "zone", "count"}` (ISO instant for `from`) and returns `{"executions": [<iso-instant>, ...]}` with `count` upcoming fire times. The parser itself is at `/app/src/main/java/com/snorkel/chronos/cron/QuartzCronExpression.java` with both methods stubbed to throw. Everything in `/app/src/main/java/com/snorkel/chronos/cron/` is yours to fill in — keep the public surface but the internal representation is your call. Rebuild with `mvn -B -DskipTests package` and the verifier will boot the jar against your implementation.
