We run a small islanded microgrid and need a dispatch plan. Candidate units (solar, wind, battery, gas/diesel) are in `/app/task_file/input_data/units.jsonl` and the grid config is in `/app/task_file/input_data/config.json`. The schema for both, the exact output shape, and every configured limit is documented in `/app/task_file/dispatcher/docs/dataflow.md`.

Finish the skeleton Rust program in `/app/task_file/dispatcher/` so that, when executed, it reads `/app/task_file/input_data/units.jsonl` and `/app/task_file/input_data/config.json` and writes `/app/task_file/output_data/dispatch.json` in the shape `/app/task_file/dispatcher/docs/dataflow.md` specifies.

A valid plan must satisfy every hard constraint that `/app/task_file/input_data/config.json` configures and `/app/task_file/scripts/model.py` enforces, including demand coverage, oversupply cap, emissions budget, renewable share, per-bus minimums, reserve requirement, feeder conflict limits, mandatory unit minimums, and thermal commitment cap. Grading is done by `/app/task_file/scripts/model.py`, which computes service balance, emissions efficiency, and grid-frequency stability.

To pass, the verifier requires `total_score >= 0.99`, `total_score_strict >= 0.92`, strong service, efficiency, and stability subscores, renewable share margin, close frequency agreement, limited degraded-unit output, and exact demand coverage on every instance. It also reruns the binary on renamed, capacity-tight alternate instances with higher renewable minimums; those dynamic checks require raw score at least `0.985` and strict score at least `0.91`. Do not modify any input file or `/app/task_file/scripts/model.py`.
